# Methodology

This page explains what each SIDSight indicator measures, why it was selected, what was left out, and how the project handles the limits of small-country data.

## Core indicators

**1. Food Import Exposure** — *Food imports (% of merchandise imports)*, World Bank / UN Comtrade.
It shows the share of merchandise imports made up of food. For SIDS, this could provide a simple view of **exposure to external supply and price shocks** (It is described as exposure, not dependency, because it reflects the structure of imports rather than the share of domestic food consumption supplied from abroad.)

**2. Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing Value Added (% of GDP)** — World Bank / national accounts.
It shows the **relative economic importance of agriculture, forestry and fisheries**. If read alongside Food Import Exposure, it creates a natural contrast: two countries can both import heavily, while one still has a substantial domestic agrifood economy and the other doesn't. Then there could be a gap worth noticing.

**3. Agricultural Land per 1,000 People** — derived from World Bank *Agricultural land (sq. km)* and population.
It provides a population-based view of agricultural land resources.
It is a **context indicator**, not a measure of food production capacity or self-sufficiency. 

*Climate context* Agricultural land data are slow-moving and generally lag by several years. Rather than treating this as a data-quality problem, SIDSight keeps the latest available year visible. The Country Brief therefore adds a separate structural indicator — **population living in low-elevation coastal zones (% of total population)** — to provide quantitative context on **geographic and climate exposure**.

**4. Digital Connectivity** — *Individuals using the Internet (% of population)*, World Bank / ITU.
It provides basic context on the **digital environment**.

It is not directly treated as a measure of digital-agriculture readiness. It is included because digital access can shape how information, services and knowledge products reach small and geographically dispersed countries.

## Fisheries, as a secondary indicator

Total fisheries production (metric tonnes) is standardized to **fisheries production per 1,000 people** for the same reason agricultural land is standardized: raw tonnage mostly reflects country size (Papua New Guinea vs. Tuvalu), not the relative importance of fisheries to that country. It appears in the Country Brief's "blue economy context" section, not on the main overview page.

## How this maps to ABAS

SIDSight is broadly framed around priorities in the Antigua and Barbuda Agenda for SIDS (2024–2034). The indicators aim to provide entry points into several recurring themes:

- food and economic resilience;
- domestic resource constraints;
- climate and geographic exposure; and
- digital connectivity.

The Country Brief and FAO Connections pages provide the policy context around these figures.

## On the use of AI in this project

AI tools were used for organizing research, and summarizing information, but not as the source of any factual data. All information collected by this repository is checked against an official source (World Bank DataBank, FAOSTAT, or fao.org) before it's used. See `digest/ai_use_note.md` for how this applies specifically to the SIDS Digest prototype.
